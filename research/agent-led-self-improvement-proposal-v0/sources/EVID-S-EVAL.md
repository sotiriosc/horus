The primary prediction concerned evidence, not reward.

Each S escape was exactly one mechanically selected first-unseen action (`ADVANCE`) at counter 3, with **zero action-model calls at the escape boundary**. Every first receipt entered the unchanged protected receipt, Measure, authorization, signed event and ModernMemory path; the selected relation became deterministic `ESTABLISHED`. No second forced action followed without a new qualifying streak. The receipt and event identities, provenance hashes, predecision assessments, selected actions and postreceipt kinds are in the [sanitized decision record](public-result.json). The 26-step archived Run C loop was the motivating observation, but no unexecuted archived outcome was imported.

The no-trigger controls behaved identically under I and S. E had established `+1` and both selected ADVANCE mechanically. F had no unseen alternative and both selected established-zero HOLD mechanically. In G, both model-selected HOLD, its authorized zero receipt moved state 0→1, and the qualifying same-state counter remained zero. The H source was closed after two qualifying fallbacks; a **fresh process** reopened signed SessionStore and ModernMemory, reconstructed suffix 2 without a persisted counter or model inference, then produced a shared third fallback before I/S forked. At the next decision I chose HOLD and S escaped. All eight pairs had identical signed event, decision, Memory and model-call prefixes before their decision boundary.

The independent replay found **zero false triggers**, no unauthorized or mismatched receipt, no predecision grounded-assessment mismatch, no action freeze/route mismatch, and no restart or matched-prefix failure. It checked all 102 per-arm decision rows, including copies of the shared signed prefix, plus the setup events. No self-review or self-diagnosis call occurred. The original incumbent source, threshold 3, canonical ordering, eligibility/reset predicates, grounded folds and protected authority path remained unchanged.

Cost and inference accounting are separate. B paid `−1` immediately and S's B post-fork total was `−1` versus I's `0`; this was the preregistered negative acquisition cost, not a prediction failure. C's +3 post-fork total reflects a useful realized outcome and two later mechanical selections; it is not evidence that an unseen action is generally beneficial. A was +1 versus 0, D was 0 versus 0, and H was +1 versus 0 within their short registered horizons. Across actual unique executions, accounting for cloned common prefixes once, the campaign made **28 logical action-model calls, 28 physical attempts, 25,181 prompt/context tokens, 258 output tokens, and 1,172.8 seconds of measured inference latency**; there were no transport failures. Five S escape boundaries avoided an action-model call. Per-arm call/token/latency data, which include inherited shared-prefix calls for replay, remain separate in the public result.

**Promotion recommendation: retain I; do not promote S yet.** The preregistered acquisition and integrity gates passed, and the rule operated in positive, negative, and neutral scheduled outcomes. Its behavioral evidence still comes from one shared C-like decision prefix and one newly sampled exact relation, with short post-fork horizons. The real B loss and unknown behavior across other states, relation types and longer runs make broad task-level benefit unestablished. Unexpected consequences observed here include downstream divergence after A's state change and mechanical exploitation after C's +1 discovery; neither alters the primary conclusion. A separate, explicitly authorized replication and promotion decision would be needed. No threshold or policy was patched after observing results.
