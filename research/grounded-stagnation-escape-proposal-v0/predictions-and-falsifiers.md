# Prospective predictions, limits, and falsifiers

These statements are frozen **before** any candidate implementation or Phase-4 observation. `I` is the untouched grounded-authority incumbent; `S` differs only by [the specified escape rule](candidate-rule.md). The primary endpoint is new authenticated exact-relation evidence, **not** total reward.

| Endpoint | Prospective prediction under matched eligible C-like contexts | What would weaken or falsify it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Primary: new exact-relation evidence acquired** | If I continues the qualifying known-zero fallback for three completed executions, S will next sample one currently `UNSEEN` exact relation and, upon successful authorized execution, create its first original receipt. Relative to an I arm that continues fallback, S will have sampled a relation I has not yet sampled at the matched decision point. | S fails to execute/admit new relation evidence despite eligible trigger; S and I have the same effective sampling pattern; or I does not produce qualifying loops in the registered matched conditions. |
| Maximum consecutive qualifying zero-fallback streak | S's completed qualifying streak should stop at three before a one-shot escape when eligibility persists; I has no corresponding bound. | S exceeds three while all trigger conditions remain true, or there is no observed streak difference under registered eligible comparisons. |
| Trigger scope and integrity | Every S trigger should meet all frozen predicates; no trigger in grounded `+1`, no-unseen, state-change, or `UNRESOLVED`-only controls. Receipt/authorization/Memory/replay results should remain exact. | Any false trigger, unauthorized receipt, altered fold/ceiling authority, or restart mismatch is a boundary failure, independent of reward. |

There is **no positive reward prediction**. The unseen relation could realize `−1`, `0`, or `+1`; all outcomes and next states must be reported as observed. S explicitly trades a known immediate neutral consequence for one missing exact-relation observation after bounded stagnation. A negative realized escape consequence is an acquisition cost, not automatically a policy failure. Its size and downstream effect matter in the later task-level assessment. Likewise, a neutral acquisition may still provide information, and a positive one is not proof the rule generally improves reward.

H1 is weakened if the registered Phase-4 comparison shows no additional relation acquisition, indistinguishable sampling, no qualifying incumbent loops, false triggers, damaged integrity, or acquisition costs that materially outweigh demonstrated information benefit under preregistered task semantics. The last judgment requires explicit cost reporting; it cannot be retrofitted by combining evidence, reward, and call counts into a post hoc score. A single archived Run C trajectory is an example of eligibility, not a substitute for matched evaluation. No hidden counterfactual outcome is imported into these predictions.
