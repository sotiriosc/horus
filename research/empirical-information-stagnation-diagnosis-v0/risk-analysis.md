# Risks and boundaries of an empirical-information candidate

The selected diagnosis asks when information coverage is missing; it does not certify an unseen action's reward. An unseen action can yield -1, change state irreversibly in another application, or impose cost beyond this bounded world. Application-specific action costs and reversibility require explicit future study controls; the current authenticated histories cannot infer them.

Mechanically detectable exclusions include no truly `UNSEEN` allowed alternative, a deterministic established +1 ceiling, a changing state or selected relation that interrupts repetition, and an active `POSSIBLE_REGIME_CHANGE` flag. A future candidate must preserve the empirical Memory fold and all receipts, acquire at most one missing relation per qualifying event, select canonically among eligible unseen actions, return to normal frozen policy, and reconstruct its eligibility after restart from signed decisions. These are proposed evaluation constraints, not implemented rules.

The broad H1/H3/H4 proxies recur on stationary-variable and single-anomaly traces. Repetition, a stable-looking empirical state, or `VARIABLE_RELATION` by itself is not evidence of harmful stagnation. A strongly positive empirical relation can make exploration unnecessary for reward; A_DETERMINISTIC and the positive stretches of B/C show why a sign guard matters, although even a positive past is not a future guarantee. The F stationary-future trace has recent sums 0 and -1 after the common ambiguous prefix but a positive cumulative sum, so a recent-only nonpositive test would prematurely alarm. Shift/restoration traces show that nonpositive recent evidence can coexist with productive regime learning; `POSSIBLE_REGIME_CHANGE` should not be treated as idle sampling. A candidate could also interrupt a negative regime's confirmation or restoration assessment.

H2's zero-crossing signs avoid those tested benign proxies, but this is retrospective discrimination on a small frozen schedule set. It can react late: R128 recent evidence first reaches 0 before D13, whereas the cumulative evidence reaches 0 only before D19. It may fail entirely where an empirical action remains slightly positive yet coverage is still scientifically valuable. It may trigger where an unseen action is costly. The source schedules lack full alternative-action assessments, so their H2 zero-hit stationary result is a **conditional signal check**, not a measured policy false-positive rate. Future Phase 3 should preregister a protected matched evaluation including alternative availability, costs, one-shot behavior, restart, and ordinary variability before interpreting benefits.
