Observation vs hypothesis boundary
Preserve a strict distinction:
AUTHENTICATED OBSERVATION

versus:
MODEL HYPOTHESIS

Observations may include:
- executed probe,
- reset state,
- observed sensor trace,
- timing,
- experiment index.
Hypotheses may include:
- suspected interaction,
- temporal bound,
- suppression/amplification,
- possible order effect,
- unresolved causal explanation.
A hypothesis can NEVER become an established relation merely because the model repeats it.
Only executed consequences constrain what is supportable.
The user's Triangulation Principle
Preserve the following operational philosophy.
A hidden truth may have many observable faces, but truthful observations must remain mutually compatible with one underlying reality.
Do not attempt to observe the hidden variable directly when controlled differences can constrain it.
Use three axes:
TIME
Ask:
- when does the effect appear?
- how long does it persist?
- does it decay?
- does order matter?
- does repetition matter?
- does prior state change the result?
Temporal evidence should constrain which explanations remain possible.
RELATION
Compare controlled contexts.
Examples:
B vs AB
AB vs BA
B vs BB
A vs AC

Use the difference in consequences to identify:
- direct effects,
- modifiers,
- suppression,
- amplification,
- interaction,
- order dependence,
- independence.
Distinguish:
no direct effect

from:
no relational effect

DIRECTION
Ask:
What distinction presently matters?

Do not reconstruct the entire world when the current objective only requires a particular distinction.
Direction means:
- unresolved question,
- competing explanations,
- target outcome.
It does not require psychological intention in the machine.
Controlled-delta principle
Prefer the smallest experiment whose difference from existing evidence can isolate something meaningful.
Examples:
B
AB

isolates the influence of preceding A.
AB
BA

isolates ordering.
B
BB

isolates repetition/state.
Longer probes should be used only when shorter contrasts cannot resolve the important remaining ambiguity.
Do NOT tell the model which contrast is correct.
Falsification principle
Once a candidate explanation exists, do not merely accumulate confirmation.
Prefer:
the smallest legal experiment whose possible outcomes distinguish the incumbent explanation from a viable alternative.

Example:
observed:
B causes X
AB does not

hypothesis:
A suppresses B

alternative:
effect depends on order

discriminating experiment:
BA

The actual outcome determines which explanations survive.
Contribution
